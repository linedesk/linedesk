# White-label partners

Agencies and IT providers run Linedesk workspaces for their clients in the shared install (Janik, 2026-11-17, LIN-322: variant A, built before go-live). This page describes what is built; slices still to come are listed at the end.

## Model

- A **partner** is its own row (`partners`), not a workspace: name, status (`pending`, `approved`, `suspended`), and its bill (`billing_account_id`, the partner bill, S2).
- Its **team** is `partner_members`: exactly one `owner` and any number of `admin`s. People join by an email invitation (`partner_invitations`, 7 days, only the sha256 of the link's token is stored). The first owner is invited by an instance admin when the partner is created.
- **Client workspaces** are normal workspaces with `organization.partner_id`. Every team member is a real `admin` member of every client workspace, marked with `member.partner_id`. So every existing tenancy check, read-sync shape and permission works unchanged for them.
- `syncPartnerMemberships` (`apps/api/src/partners.ts`) keeps those memberships equal to "every team member in every client workspace", none while the partner is suspended. It runs in the transaction of each change (join, removal, new client, suspend, approve). A membership that goes is removed like any removed member's (`forgetMember`: API keys revoked, setup links ended), and the workspace invitations that person sent there are canceled. A team member who is in a client workspace on their own keeps that membership untouched.
- Removing someone from the team does not undo what they set up in a client workspace (webhooks, call-email recipients, connected calendars): the partner owner checks that when they remove someone, as for any removed workspace admin.

## Permissions

- The `partner` permission is no role's. A **session** gets it when the person is on a partner team, or holds an open invitation to one (so a new account can open the link). Never while impersonated, never with an API key, a personal key, a setup link or the agent.
- Every partner action names its partner. The handler checks that the caller is on that partner's team; anyone else gets 403 (the same answer whether the partner exists or not). Owner-only: rename, invite, withdraw an invitation, remove a member. A suspended partner changes nothing.
- A membership that comes from a partner is changed only on the partner: `team.setRole`, `team.removeMember`, `team.transferOwnership`, `team.leave` and Server admin's `membership.setRole` / `membership.remove` refuse it (`partner_managed`). The database allows a partner membership only as `admin` (`member_partner_admin`).
- Server admin › Partners (`partner.adminList`, `partner.create`, `partner.approve`, `partner.suspend`, `instance:admin`) lists, creates, approves and suspends partners. Suspension removes the team from every client workspace at once; approval brings them back. A partner from self-serve sign-up emails every instance admin once its sign-up is committed (`billing.alert` job `partner_signup`: company, owner's email, interval, a link to `#admin/partners/waiting`; nothing when it is approved or gone by then). The page's "Waiting for approval" tab (`#admin/partners/waiting`) lists the pending partners with their number. The partner area's note says approval turns on the brand in clients' emails and, once available, the own domain, and that the plan and client workspaces do not need it; it promises no time.
- Nobody in a client workspace through a partner makes anyone admin there, by invitation or role change (`partner_admin_grant`): an admin added that way could be a second account of their own that keeps the workspace after they leave the team. The client's staff join as members; the client's own owner makes admins.
- **The client's owner** (S2): only the partner's owner invites it (`workspace.inviteClientOwner`, or `ownerEmail` on `workspace.createForClient`), by a workspace invitation with role `owner`. Never someone on the partner team (`owner_on_team`), never a second owner (`client_has_owner`); and the owner of a client, or someone invited as one, never joins the partner team (`client_owner`, at the team invitation and at its accept). The owner can remove the partner, so a partner admin must not hand that to an account of their own.
- **Who else is in a client workspace** (security review of #1100, N-1): the partner's owner sees, per client, every member who is not there through the team, with their role and who invited them (`partner.get`, `clients[].otherMembers`). Admins of the team see the client's owner only.
- An account on a partner team is never deleted with a workspace or a removal (`membersOnlyHere`, `deleteAccountWithoutWorkspace`): the partner area is its place without a workspace.
- Every partner write is in the audit log under the kind "Partners".
- Known gap (security review of #1100, L-1): the `partner` permission also goes to a session whose address holds an open invitation, so such a person (no workspace yet) is an actor with `partner` and `account:own`. They reach no workspace data and no other partner (every partner action checks the team); only their own invitation and their own account settings.

A note above says when the bill waits for its Checkout ("Activate White-label Cloud" with the base fee and the price per client workspace at the bill's interval and the draft cap; its button resumes the setup's one Activate step, below) or does not run. Before the Checkout, New client makes drafts up to the cap (then it is greyed out with the reason), the New client dialog has no owner email and ends with "Open workspace" instead of the number, the client rows offer no owner invitation, and Team's invite form is off ("Invitations go out once you activate White-label Cloud"). A partner member without any workspace lands there. The workspace menu has "Partner: <name>".
- **Sign-up** `/signup?plan=partner&interval=month|year` (the website's White-label Cloud "Become a partner"): like Cloud's, with its own first screen ("Start White-label Cloud", what it is for; no free trial), then your name and the company's name (the partner's name and the partner bill's Stripe customer) and **Create account**, which goes on to the partner owner's **agent setup** (`#partner/agent`, below): **setup before payment** (Janik, 2026-11-18), like the Cloud trial's setup before the card. The owner sets up the company, brand draft and up to `UNPAID_PARTNER_CLIENTS` (5) draft client workspaces first, a limit for the setup only (more follow after the Checkout); **"Activate White-label Cloud"** (the last row on the agent page, the note in the partner area, the last step by hand) opens the setup's one Activate step, whose button opens Stripe Checkout, charged at once with no trial: the base fee and a seat per draft (`planItems`). Checkout comes back to `/billing/return?…&partner=<id>`, which confirms for the partner (the bill and its drafts go `active`) and goes on to `#partner/agent`, or to the partner area once they chose "Set up by hand" for this partner in this browser; a canceled Checkout returns to the partner area.
- **Client workspace**: the Team page lists the workspace's own people first and the partner team's memberships after them, each with a badge naming the partner (no role menu, no remove, no Edit: their account is the partner's). Danger zone offers them no Leave, only where their access changes (the partner area). Workspace settings › Danger zone has "Remove partner…" for the owner, from the workspace's partner (`team.list` `partner`), so also while the partner is suspended and its team has no memberships there. Billing shows the client's owners and admins their minutes only, no amounts (`billing.workspaceUsage`, `minutesOnly`): the partner sets what it charges its client. The partner's admins read there that the workspace is on their partner plan (`/v1/me` `workspace.viaPartner`, their membership comes from the team); the partner's owner gets the partner bill's page. A team member without a name is asked for it in the partner area, never in a client workspace.
- **Partner › Billing** shows the plan in the shared plan card (ui/PlanCard.tsx, as on Activate): the partner base fee a month with the client workspaces next to it ("$14 a month + $9 a month × 2 client workspaces", "$384 billed yearly" as the grey note), each client workspace at the Cloud seat price (`billing.overview` `kind: 'partner'`, `clientSeatCents`, both as the subscription charges them, else the install's prices).
- **Invitation page** (`#partner-invite=<token>`): after sign-in (a new address gets its account with an email code; the invitation lets it in, `signup-gate.ts`, and keeps it, `joinOrUndo`), Join. An account made for an invitation that ends unused goes with the sign-up sweep.
- **Server admin › Partners**.

## Agent setup for the partner owner (2026-11-18)

Agent-first, manual second, like a business's onboarding (docs/billing.md section 3.2). Right after the partner sign-up (before any payment) the owner lands on `#partner/agent` (`partner/PartnerAgentSetup.tsx`, the agent setup page with `scope="partner"`): a setup link, the agent pickers, the prompt and the progress, with "Set up by hand in the partner area" as one small link under the card (remembered per partner in this browser, `setup-choice.ts`). The partner area's top bar has "Set up with an AI agent" back to it, for the owner only.

**One flow, Activate at its end** (Janik, 2026-11-18): sign-up, the agency (company name, brand), the clients (none to `UNPAID_PARTNER_CLIENTS` drafts), Activate (the end of the setup), and after the Checkout a number for each client, then invitations. The agent page and the steps by hand both follow it:

- **Agent page** (`#partner/agent`, agent-first, the default): its rows come in that order. Before the Checkout the partner's Team row waits (invitations go out only after it, `partnerOrder` in AgentSetup.tsx) and "Activate White-label Cloud" is the very last row, below the picked client's steps. Its button opens the Activate step below; Back there returns to the agent page.
- **By hand** (`#partner/setup/<step>`, `partner/PartnerSetup.tsx`, the fallback): Your agency, then **Clients** (`clients`): each draft is a row with "Set up with the agent" (the default: the agent page with that client picked) and "Set up by hand" (the client's own onboarding: the free steps only, `partnerDraftSteps`; its last Continue and "Finish later" come back to this step, and the client resumes at the number after the Checkout). "Add client" is the partner area's New client dialog while under the cap; with none, "Skip, add clients later" goes on. Then **Activate** (`activate`, only while the bill waits for its owner's Checkout).
- **The draft limit is said as a setup limit**, in one short line ("Add up to 5 clients now. You can add more after you activate.", `team.partner.clients.setupLimit`): the Clients step's line, under the agent page's Client workspaces row before the Checkout, and in the partner area's Clients section at the cap. At the cap Add client and New client are greyed with the same words.
- **The Activate step** is one screen for all three ways in (the agent page's row, the partner area's note, the Clients step; `openPartnerActivate` remembers where from for Back, `#partner/setup/activate`): the drafts, each with a small Remove (a light confirm, then `partner.deleteClient`: the owner's, audited, a draft has no owner), then the shared plan card (ui/PlanCard.tsx; the price leads, a partner has no trial): the price a month first ("$14 a month + $9 a month × 2 client workspaces"; no drafts: "+ $9 a month per client workspace") and what Checkout charges today as the grey note ("$384 billed yearly, charged today", ui/price.tsx), "Each client workspace includes:" with "A phone number", "50 call minutes each month, then 5¢ a minute" and "5 calls at the same time", the tax line and "See all details" (`partner.get` `bill.prices`), and only Back and "Activate White-label Cloud". No way off to the agent or a client there. Closing the page leaves it in the partner area's Activate note, which resumes it.

- **The link** (`agentSetup.createPartnerKey {partnerId}`, `agentSetup.revokePartner`): the partner's owner only, signed in as themselves within the last 10 minutes, with a confirmed address; never a partner admin, through a key or while impersonated. One per person (a new link ends their others), 4 hours, tied to the session that made it (`agent_setup_keys.partner_id`, migration 0157). It ends when the partner is suspended, the owner leaves the partner or their account is disabled (`partner_suspended`, `left_partner`, `account_disabled`).
- **What the agent may do** (`PARTNER_KEY_PARTNER_ACTIONS`, `PARTNER_KEY_WORKSPACE_ACTIONS` in `apps/api/src/agent-setup.ts`): the partner's company name (`partner.update`), its brand draft (`partnerBrand.get`/`set`, reviewed by the instance admins as always), inviting team members (`partnerMember.invite`, `revokeInvite`), how far it got (`partner.setSetupStep`, `partners.setup_step`, never back), and per client workspace the business, assistant, pick-up times and notifications. The MCP tools for the partner never take `partnerId`: the server fills in the key's partner.
- **Never the agent, the owner on the page:** adding a client workspace (a seat on the partner bill: the Client workspaces row shows its price, "Add client" opens the partner area's New client dialog), a number ("Add number" opens the client's Phone number settings), call forwarding and the test call (as on the install's page), anything on the bill or its payment, adding seats, the partner's own address and its approval (instance admins approve; S5 is not built), and removing people or clients. `runAction` refuses a partner key `billing:manage`, `workspace:own`, `team:write` and `instance:admin`, any action outside the two lists, another partner, and any workspace that is not one of this partner's client workspaces (the owner's own business included).
- `setup_status` for a partner key: the partner's steps (company, brand, team, client workspaces; the last is the owner's row until the first client) and then each client workspace's, with the seat price and the setup page in the texts.
- `GET /v1/partner/agent-setup?partnerId=&workspace=`: the page's state (the owner's link, never the key, and the progress), for the partner's owner signed in as themselves; main address only.

## Partner bill (S2, LIN-453)

- `account.signUp` with plan `partner` makes the partner (`pending`), the caller its owner, and the partner bill (`kind = 'partner'`, `pending_payment`, owned by the partner's owner). No workspace. One partner per owner (`already_partner`); a sign-up waiting for Checkout is answered again, like Cloud's. The sign-up sweep deletes a partner sign-up that never finished Checkout with its partner.
- Checkout for the partner bill sells the partner base fee alone (`partner_monthly` or `partner_yearly`, quantity 1): Stripe Checkout takes no line item with quantity 0. The first client workspace adds the seat item (`ws_monthly` or `ws_yearly`) to the subscription, every later one raises its quantity. Partner › Clients sends `workspace.createForClient` with an idempotency key kept until a definite answer, so a retry after a timeout gets the first client back, never a second one (security review of #1113, L-3).
- **The bill is the gate**, not approval: a client workspace goes on the partner bill once it runs (`active`, `past_due`, `comped`); a bill that does not run: `bill_inactive`. White-label Cloud has no free trial (Janik, 2026-11-18): the partner pays at its Checkout and can add clients right after, with no limit.
- **Before the Checkout (setup before payment, 2026-11-18):** `workspace.createForClient` puts at most `UNPAID_PARTNER_CLIENTS` (5, was 3 until 2026-11-18; `packages/actions/src/partner.ts`) draft client workspaces on the bill waiting for its Checkout (`billForNewClient` with `draft`; more: `unpaid_client_limit`). A draft is `pending_payment` like a Cloud sign-up's workspace and gets exactly what that blocks before the card (`REFUSED_WHILE_PENDING_PAYMENT`: no number, no forwarding check, no team invitation, no webhooks or integrations, no voice cloning or line lookup; browser test calls and business lookups within the setup allowance), said as "Activate White-label Cloud first" (`server.api.partner.activateFirst`). It has no card step of its own (`activate` is only for a workspace's own trial, `waitsForOwnTrial`), and no seat is synced (the Checkout sells the seats). Nothing goes out either: no client owner invitation (`workspace.inviteClientOwner`, `createForClient` with `ownerEmail`) and no partner team invitation (`partnerMember.invite`), each `activate_first`; the partner's setup link has the same limits (its actions run through the same handlers). Adding a client after the Checkout, or by a server admin's move, still needs a running bill (`bill_not_started` for a move onto a waiting bill). The sign-up sweep deletes a partner that never paid with its drafts.
- **Approval** stays independent of payment, as before: the instance admins are emailed at sign-up (`partner_signup`) and may approve before or after the Checkout; a partner waiting for approval works with its clients, and approval gates its brand (S4) and own domain (the brand also needs a paid invoice).
- Seats follow after the commit (`seatsFollow`, `apps/api/src/partner-billing.ts`), never inside the request: the change queues a `partner.seatSync` job in its own transaction, so Stripe being down holds neither the request nor a database connection (security review of #1113, L-3). Each sync sets the seat item to the client workspaces on the bill now, one sync per bill at a time, and the proration follows what changes in Stripe (the seats the subscription has against the count), never the event that started it (security review of #1109, L-1): more seats are charged (yearly: invoiced now; monthly: prorated on the next invoice), fewer are not refunded. A failed sync is tried again by the job runner (5 more tries with backoff, from 30 s to about 8 min); after the last try the instance admins get an email. Each step of a try ends within 100 s (each Stripe request within 8 s), well before the job's 5-minute expiry, and a job that still ends failed (expired, the worker stopped) goes to the dead-letter queue `partner.seatSyncFailed`, which emails them too (security review of #1115, L-2). The nightly reconciliation reports the seat quantity until the next change sets it.
- The partner's owner manages the partner bill with `partnerId` on the billing actions (`billing.overview`, `state`, `startCheckout`, `confirmCheckout`, `startTopUp`, `cancel`, `resume`, `setAutoRecharge`, `setRechargeCap`, `setExtraCalls`, `openPortal`, `retryRecharge`). A call that names `partnerId` needs the `partner` permission, from any workspace or none; every handler checks the caller owns that partner and the bill (`partnerBillOf`), else 403 whatever the install's billing. `billing:manage` itself the partner's owner holds only in a client workspace on their bill that is not disabled, where they are an admin (security review of #1109, M-1). Raising an assistant to Premium or Ultra: the bill's owner; never in a disabled workspace. In a client workspace only the partner's owner raises it; anyone who edits the assistant lowers it (S3, LIN-440).
- A partner bill is never handed over (`billing.transferOwner`, a workspace's hand-over) and takes no other workspace; a client workspace never leaves it by `billing.moveWorkspace`. It leaves only with its partner:
  - **Server admin › Workspaces › Partner…** (`partner.adminMoveClient`, `instance:admin`, LIN-530): an instance admin moves a workspace to another partner, adds a workspace to a partner, or takes it from its partner (`toPartnerId` null), with the workspace's name typed and a reason. The owner, data, numbers and calls stay. The old team leaves as on a release (memberships, their API keys and setup links, the invitations they sent) and the new team becomes admin (`syncPartnerMemberships`). Onto a partner: the gates of a new client there (`billForNewClient`: a bill that runs, after its Checkout; without billing an approved partner under the install's ceiling), the workspace goes on the new partner's bill, the new bill's seats rise and the old bill's drop after the commit (`seatsFollow`, both `partner.seatSync` jobs; §6 of billing.md), its assistants keep their call quality (LIN-589), and its extra calls and usage limit stay behind. Taken from its partner: as `partner.releaseClient` (a new Cloud bill of its owner, `pending_payment`; refused without an owner, `client_no_owner`). Refused: the same partner (`same_partner`), a suspended partner, the workspace's owner on the new team (`owner_on_team`), its bill past due or suspended (`client_bill_inactive`), calls running, and while impersonating. Where a bill is involved it needs an instance admin signed in with their passkey (`refuseMoneyChange`, as `billing.moveWorkspace`); without billing it also runs with a personal key (MCP, CLI). No email; the audit log files it under Partners.
  - `partner.releaseClient` (the partner's owner, the workspace's name typed; the client needs its owner first: `client_no_owner`) and `workspace.removePartner` (the client's owner, the name typed). Refused while calls run there (`calls_running`).
  - The team leaves at once (memberships removed with their API keys and setup links, the invitations they sent canceled). People the team invited into the workspace (whose latest accepted invitation there came from someone on the team) are listed in both confirmations, and the person picks who of them leaves too; nobody is picked by default, and the action takes the picked member ids (`removeMemberIds`), each checked. The workspace's owner and anyone who ever owned it (invited as owner, or on either side of an ownership transfer) are never listed. The audit entry names each address removed (security reviews of #1109, L-4, and #1113, M-1). The usage limit goes. The workspace moves to a new Cloud bill of its owner, `pending_payment`, read with the owner row locked: an ownership transfer at the same moment waits or is seen, so the bill is never the former owner's and the owner never leaves with the picked people (security review of #1113, N-1), with the partner bill's interval: calls are answered again once they add a card. Its assistants keep their call quality (LIN-589). Its extra calls at the same time stay behind (0, as a Server admin move onto a bill without a subscription). The partner's seats and extra-calls item drop after the commit, never refunded. A deleted workspace's extra calls leave its bill's extra-calls item the same way.
  - The sign-up sweep never deletes a workspace older than its bill (one that moved there).
- `partner.deleteClient` (the partner's owner, the workspace's name typed, never while impersonated, no key) deletes a client workspace nobody owns yet (none, or only invited; one with its owner: `client_has_owner`, hand it over instead) through the same path as Delete workspace (`seatsFollowDelete`, then `deleteWorkspace`): every row, the recordings, the numbers at the carrier, a pending owner invitation, and the accounts of people the team invited who are in no other workspace; never an account on the partner team. Its seat drops after the commit, never refunded. Everyone else in it is emailed (the partner's brand). One audit entry (`partners`). No fresh sign-in, like the hand-over: nobody owns the workspace yet. Partner › Clients offers it in the row's menu, after Invite owner or the owner's invitation.

Without billing (self-host, dev, staging without Stripe) and without a partner bill, client workspaces work without a bill, up to a limit per partner (25 by default; Server admin › Partners, `partner.setClientLimit`), and only once the partner is approved. A partner a server admin made by hand on a billed install has no bill and makes no client workspace (`client_needs_bill`).

## Partner › Clients and usage limits (S3, LIN-454)

- **Clients** lists every client workspace of the partner (`partner.listClients`, the team's owner and admins; 403 for anyone else, with no key): owner, number, whether calls reach it (forwarding checked, not needed for a new or ported number, or not yet), the last call from a real caller, and this period's calls, minutes and cost. Counts and money only, never call content. The period is the partner bill's current one; minutes and cost are the ledger's (usage, numbers and adjustments posted for the workspace on the partner bill), the same sum the limit measures. Off billing: the UTC month, without cost.
- Problems come first, the worst first: plan not active, workspace disabled, limit reached, near its limit (80 %), no number, forwarding failed, forwarding not checked, provider errors in the last 7 days.
- **Monthly usage limit** per client (`partner.setClientUsageLimit`; $1 to $100,000, empty for none; only a client on the partner bill, `client_not_billed`): what the client may use from the partner bill in the bill's period (`organization.usage_limit_cents`, `apps/api/src/usage-limit.ts`). The partner's owner sets any value; an admin of the team may only lower it or set one where there is none (security review of #1113, L-1). At 80 % and 100 % the partner team gets an email, once each per period whatever the limit is changed to (`usage_limit_alerts`, job `usage.limitAlert`). At the limit a new phone call is refused (`usage_limit`) with the usual refused-call line and filed as missed with "Call back".
- The limit is soft (security review of #1113, L-2): it is checked only when a call starts, against what is posted to the ledger. A running call is never cut, and calls that started before the limit was reached finish and count afterwards, so the limit can be passed by them (at most the calls at the same time times their length).
- **Call quality** in a client (decisions.md 2026-11-18, replacing LIN-440's "the partner's owner only"): the partner's team (owner and admins) changes an assistant's level and models in the client workspace's assistant settings. Per client the partner decides whether the client's own owner and admins may change it too: Partner › Clients › Settings › "Client can change call quality" (`workspace_settings.client_call_quality`, `partner.setClientCallQuality`; the partner's owner turns it on, anyone on the team turns it off; audited). Off by default: the client's people then see no call quality block at all (`voice.options` `callQuality.mayChange` false) and the API refuses any change of the level or its models from them (`refuseLevelChange`, `apps/api/src/level-raise.ts`); a voice of another provider stays theirs. The client's members never change it. A client that changes partner has it off again (`ownKeysOff`). There are no Allow switches (call quality 4/5). The client's people see no prices: the level options, the workspace settings, `/v1/me` and Phone numbers show none (`partnerManaged`), and a spending-cap email goes to the partner's owner instead of the client's owners (security review of #1109, L-2).

## Partner brand (S4, LIN-455)

- **Partner › Brand** (the partner's owner; the team sees it read-only): product name, logo (PNG, checked like the install's: `decodeLogo`, at most 512×512, no SVG), color, support email, terms and privacy links (https only). Previews of the sign-in page and an email. Stored in `partner_branding` (one row per partner); `partnerBrand.get` / `partnerBrand.set` (`apps/api/src/partner-brand.ts`).
- **Protected names** (Server admin › Partners, `partnerBrand.protectedNames` / `partnerBrand.setProtectedNames`, instance setting `partner.protectedNames`; built-in list `DEFAULT_PROTECTED_NAMES` in `packages/actions/src/partner-brand.ts`): a product name that is, or contains, one is refused (`name_protected`). Case, accents, punctuation inside a word ("I.R.S.", "Vi-sa"), look-alike digits ("L1nedesk"), Cyrillic, Greek, Armenian and Cherokee look-alike letters ("Сhase" with a Cyrillic С), ø, ł, đ, "rn" for "m", "vv" for "w" and full-width letters are ignored; dots, hyphens and other punctuation split words ("Chase.com", "IRS-Help"; security review of #1111, N-3); a protected name counts when words in a row spell it, and names of six letters or more also count glued into a longer word. Invisible characters (soft hyphen, zero-width spaces and joiners, every Unicode format character) are refused in any product name, the install's too. Saved brands stay when the list changes; the next change is checked, and approve checks the name again. The page warns when the list is empty.
- **Review** (security review of #1111, M-2): the brand has a draft and an approved version. Every change of name, logo, support email or links is a new revision that waits until an instance admin approves it in Server admin › Partners › Brands (`partnerBrand.reviews`, `partnerBrand.approve` with the revision and content hash they saw and a reason); until then clients and emails keep the approved version (none approved: the install's). The revision only goes up, also when a change goes back to the approved content (that needs no review: the state comes from the content, not the numbers). Approve must name both the current revision and the hash of the content the admin saw (name, the logo's bytes, support email, links), so an admin never approves a version they did not see (security review of #1111, N-1); it also checks the name against the protected names as they are then. The color is not reviewed. Every waiting change is emailed to the instance admins with the new name, logo address, support email and links. `partnerBrand.reset` (with a reason) removes a brand at once, without suspending the partner. All three are in the audit log ("Partners").
- **In the app**: `GET /v1/branding` answers a signed-in session with the brand of its active workspace when that is a client of a partner that is not suspended (`brandingForActor`): the partner's team sees its draft, everyone else the approved version; otherwise the install's. The web app asks again when it switches workspace. A partner's clients see no "Powered by" line and no star prompt; the workspace menu's terms and privacy links are the partner's. The logo is served at `/v1/branding/partners/<id>/logo.png?v=<version>` (PNG, `nosniff`, `default-src 'none'`), only for a version the brand has: the approved logo to anyone, cached for good (`immutable`, a new version gets a new address); a draft logo only to the partner's team and instance admins, never cached (`private, no-store`; security review of #1111, N-2).

## Own address (S5, LIN-456)

Built: PR 5.1, how the API tells addresses apart; PR 5.2, sessions bound to their address; PR 5.0, Caddy's partner site; PR 5.3, the brand and links by address; PR 5.4, Partner › Domain with Cloudflare for SaaS. It stays dark until a server admin enters the Cloudflare for SaaS token in Server admin › Providers › Partner addresses.

- **`partner_domains`**: one row per address (lower-case host name, no port, no trailing dot), status `pending`, `verifying`, `active`, `failed`, `stopped` or `removed`; at most one not-removed address per partner and per host name. Removing sets `removed`, the row stays. Not synced to browsers.
- **Which address a request came in on** (`apps/api/src/request-host.ts`, `resolveHost`): the install's own (the hosts of `WEB_ORIGIN`, `PUBLIC_URL`, `BETTER_AUTH_URL`, `LINEDESK_DEV_ORIGINS`), a partner's address while it is `active` and its partner is `approved` (not pending, not suspended), or unknown. Each request is resolved once, in the host middleware; the guard, the trusted origins and Better Auth all use that one answer, so an address that goes out while a request is under way cannot leave that request half on the partner's address and half on the install's own.

From then on, an unknown host gets 421 `unknown_host` on every route but `/healthz`; the web app shows one line, "This address is not set up.", with a link to the install's own address.
- **On a partner's address**, what belongs to the install's own address is 404 `main_host_only`, also for a server admin: Server admin (`/v1/admin/*`, every `instance:admin` action), the partner area (`partner` actions, `partner*.` actions, billing actions for the partner bill), impersonation start, MCP, OAuth and their `/.well-known` documents, the API description, self-serve sign-up (`/v1/signup*`, `account.signUp`), New workspace (`POST /v1/workspaces`, `workspace.create`), AI agent setup links, Stripe Checkout and portal starts (`billing.startCheckout`, `confirmCheckout`, `startTopUp`, `openPortal`), the first admin's setup, and the webhooks and demo calls other services send. - **`GET /v1/health/host?nonce=<fresh random>`** on a partner's address that is not removed (also before it is live): its id and `signWithSecretsKey('host-probe', id + nonce)`, so S5's check can prove the address reaches this server now and an old answer cannot be replayed (`hostProbePassed`). - **Caddy's site for partner traffic** (PR 5.0, LIN-558): the routes are one snippet, `(app-routes)` in docker/Caddyfile, that the install's own site and the partner site import alike, so a partner address gets the same headers and the same public entry (`import api public`: a client's own `X-Linedesk-Entry: internal` is replaced, `caddy-partner-site.test.ts`). ### Cloudflare for SaaS: the steps done by hand (Janik or Tim)

Nothing in the app creates or changes anything at Cloudflare until PR 5.4 ships and its token is entered; these steps are done in the Cloudflare dashboard, on staging first. No code reads the Keychain. State on 2026-11-18: steps 1 to 4 done (`saas-origin.linedesk.ai`, `partners.linedesk.ai` and the staging names exist), the certificate of step 5 is issued (not on prod yet), D1 is decided (Full (strict), option a); prod enablement: runbook.md, "Prod enablement checklist".

1. **Turn it on**: zone `linedesk.ai` › SSL/TLS › Custom Hostnames › enable Cloudflare for SaaS (100 custom hostnames free on every plan, then $0.10 each a month).
2. **Origin record**: DNS › a proxied A record `saas-origin.linedesk.ai` → the prod server (`saas-origin-staging.example.com` → the staging server for the staging run).
3. **Fallback origin**: SSL/TLS › Custom Hostnames › Fallback Origin = `saas-origin.linedesk.ai`; wait until it shows Active.
4. **Target for partners**: DNS › a proxied CNAME `partners.linedesk.ai` → `saas-origin.linedesk.ai` (`partners-staging.example.com` for staging). Partners point their address here.
5. **Origin certificate**: re-issue the Cloudflare Origin CA certificate so it also covers `saas-origin.linedesk.ai` (and the staging name), and replace it on the servers (Caddy). Decision D1 (tried on staging 2026-11-18): Full (strict) validates for a partner host with the custom origin, because Cloudflare checks the certificate against the custom origin's name. No Configuration Rule; Authenticated Origin Pulls is optional hardening.
6. **Token** (PR 5.4 is built): an API token limited to zone `linedesk.ai` with Zone › SSL and Certificates › Edit (custom hostnames); the app needs nothing else. Keep it in the Keychain (`linedesk-cloudflare` / `custom-hostnames`), and paste it on each server (staging, then prod) into Server admin › Providers › Partner addresses › Cloudflare for SaaS, with the zone ID, the CNAME target (`partners.linedesk.ai`) and the custom origin (`saas-origin.linedesk.ai`).

What a partner adds at its own DNS host for `app.theiragency.com` (the Domain page lists them with copy buttons, PR 5.4): `TXT _linedesk.app.theiragency.com` = `linedesk-verify=<token>` (proves the claim; a fresh token per add, so an old CNAME left behind by a former partner never passes) and `CNAME app.theiragency.com` → `partners.linedesk.ai`. Only once the TXT resolves does the app create the custom hostname at Cloudflare, with HTTP validation of a DV certificate (Cloudflare's validation passes on its own once the CNAME routes to us). When the name sits in another Cloudflare account, Cloudflare asks for an ownership TXT too, which the page then shows. The address goes live when Cloudflare says active for the hostname and its certificate and the app's own probe (`/v1/health/host`) answers through it.

## Own AI keys (S7, LIN-458)

Optional. A partner on Linedesk Cloud can run its clients' calls on its own provider accounts; a call that ran fully on them has no usage fee. Nothing is charged for using them, and there is no trial. Without them every call runs on the install's keys and is billed as before.

- **Partner › AI keys** (only where the partner has a bill; the owner changes, the team sees): one key per stage, speech to text, language model, voice and realtime model, from the install's provider registry (all key-only). Stored in `partner_providers` (its own table, never `provider_settings`, so no query of the install's keys can pick one up), AES-256-GCM bound to the slot `partner:<partnerId>:<stage>:<provider>.api_key`; `rotate-secrets-key.ts` re-encrypts them. Reads give the provider, the last four characters and the test, never a key. Setting a key needs the personal key step-up (a sign-in or passkey from the last 10 minutes). Each key is tested at once (the free check, `provider-test.ts`, asked after the partner row's lock is let go) and daily (the health check job; not for a suspended partner or one an instance admin turned off); `partnerKeys.test` is limited to 10 per 10 minutes per partner. A failing key is marked, its error text masked (`maskFailure`: the key and anything that looks like one) and the owner is emailed at most once a day.
- **Per client** (Partner › Clients › Settings…, the owner only, clients on the partner bill): `partner.setClientOwnKeys` sets `workspace_settings.partner_keys`. The client list shows an "Own keys" badge. A client that changes partner (release, removal by its owner, a Server admin move in, out or between partners) has the switch turned off: only the new partner's owner turns it on again (security review of #1289/#1290, M-1).
- **The forecast** at `call.start` (`workspaceOwnKeys`, called by `assistantForCall`): `own_keys` only when the switch is on, the partner is approved and its keys are not turned off by an instance admin, the client's calls come in on its own Twilio or Telnyx account (not the install's carrier), every stage of the call's pipeline has a key that tested ok, the assistant's voice is of the partner's voice provider, and the voice is not a clone (clones live in the install's voice account). Anything else: the call's level, billed. `calls.partner_keys` records it.
- **The agent** reads the keys of that one call (`GET /v1/agent/call-provider-config?callId=`, install agent token only, a call token is refused, 404 unless the call runs and is on partner keys). It builds the call on the partner's keys alone, with no environment key, no install backup voice and no other voice provider of the install; if the keys cannot cover the call it falls back to the install's config. `call.finish` reports `keySources` per stage; the call is $0 (`price_basis: own_keys`) only when the forecast said so and every stage of the pipeline reports `partner`. A missing or partial report, or the stale sweep, bills at the call's level (fail closed). A voice failure marks the partner's key failing instead of the install's provider only when the agent reports that the partner's key served that stage (`call.reportVoiceFailure` `keySource: 'partner'`); a stage that fell back to the install's keys, or a report without a source, is the install's provider failure (security review of #1289/#1290, L-4).
- **Kill switch** (Server admin › Partners, `partnerKeys.adminDisable` with a reason, `partnerKeys.adminEnable`): calls go back to the install's keys and are billed at once, the keys cannot be changed, the owner is emailed with the reason.
- **Scope**: every action checks the partner team (`partnerOnTeam`), writes are the owner's; the partner agent setup key is refused (not in `PARTNER_KEY_PARTNER_ACTIONS`), personal keys are refused (`personal-key-scope.ts`), and on a partner's own address they are main-host only. All writes are in the audit log ("Partners").
- **Privacy**: a switched-on client's call audio and text go to the partner's providers under the partner's own accounts and terms (the partner is responsible for them); the install stores the encrypted keys, the last four characters, test results and masked error texts.
- **Open points**: own-keys calls get no install after-call transcript (the partner has no transcript stage of its own yet) and no install backup voice; turning the switch off only makes calls billed again, it does not move the assistant to another voice.

## Own sender (S6, LIN-457)

A partner's clients' emails can come from an address on the partner's domain (`notify@mail.theiragency.com`) instead of the install's. Optional; billing emails to the partner's owner and admin alerts never use it.

- **Partner › Email sender** (only where the partner has a bill; the owner changes, the team sees): one address. Adding it needs the brand approved and the first invoice paid (`brandGate`, as the brand in emails; else `sender_locked`) and an install whose email provider can verify domains (else `sender_unavailable` and the section says so). The domain is created at the install's email provider (Resend, `apps/api/src/domains/sender.ts`), which returns the DNS records (DKIM TXT, return-path MX and TXT) the page lists with copy buttons; the partner adds them at its DNS host. Stored in `partner_sender_domains` (one not-removed row per partner and per domain); actions in `packages/actions/src/partner-sender.ts`, server in `apps/api/src/partner-sender.ts`.
- **States**: `pending` (records not found yet), `verified`, `failed`, `stopped` (pending for 72 hours; "Check now" checks again), `removed` (the row stays). A claim ends on its own (security review of #1291, L-1 and L-3): one that never verified 7 days after it was added ("Check now" never extends that), one that verified once and then failed 30 days after it failed, and a suspended partner's right after the suspension (the job retries one Resend refused, and never checks a suspended partner's domain). Checks: "Check now" (10 per 10 minutes per partner), and the job `partner.sender-check` every 5 minutes: a new domain every 5 minutes for the first hour, then every 30 minutes up to 72 hours; a verified one once a day. A verified domain turns `failed` only after two failed checks in a row (one DNS hiccup is not enough); the owner is emailed when it is verified, when it failed and when a server admin removed it.
- **Sending**: `emailBrandOf` gives the From address only while the domain is `verified` at the provider the install sends with now (`verifiedSenderAddress`: not after a switch away from Resend or without the domains key, security review of #1291, L-2); anything else sends from the install's address with the partner's name, as S4 did. An email is never held back over its sender. "Send test email" (verified only, 5 an hour) sends one email from the address to the caller's own account email.
- **Refused domains**: the install's own (`WEB_ORIGIN`, `PUBLIC_URL` and their subdomains, `domain_install`), a domain with a protected name in a label (as brand names, punycode labels decoded first so look-alikes are folded, `domain_protected`; one that does not decode is `address_invalid`), and one another partner holds (`domain_taken`). The local part is letters, digits, `.`, `_` and `-` (at most 32).
- **Reply-To** (security review of #1111, L-3): the partner's approved support email is Reply-To only after its owner proved it with a 6-digit code sent there (`partnerBrand.verifySupportEmail`, `partnerBrand.confirmSupportEmail`; Better Auth's `verification` table, the code hashed, 10 minutes, 3 tries, 5 codes an hour). `partner_branding.support_email_verified` holds the proven address, so a new approved support email needs the code again. Until then the partner's emails have no Reply-To. Security notices (an admin opened the account or made a key in it) never carry it (`securityNoticeBrand`).
- **Server admin › Partners**: the partner's row shows "Own sender" (or "not verified") with the address; "Remove sender…" (`partnerSender.adminRemove`, with a reason the owner is emailed) switches back to the install's address at once and deletes the domain at the provider. A new sender emails the instance admins.
- **Scope**: every action checks the partner team, writes are the owner's; the partner agent setup key and personal keys are refused, and on a partner's own address they are main-host only. All writes are in the audit log ("Partners").
- **Setting up an install** (Server admin › Providers › Resend): partner senders need the sending key on "All domains" (Resend checks the From domain against the key) plus a second key with full access in **Domains API key (partners)** (`RESEND_DOMAINS_API_KEY`), used only to create, check and remove partner domains. Without it, or with SMTP, the section shows that the feature is not on this server. Resend's free and Pro plans include 10 domains (the install's counts); more needs Resend's domains add-on. `SENDER_DOMAINS=fake` turns on a fake provider for local runs and tests (a domain whose first label is `pending` or `failing` stays so).
- **Privacy**: the partner's sender domain and its DNS records are stored by the install and at Resend; the support email address receives a verification code.
- **Open points**: no DMARC hint on the page; no check that the install's sending key covers all domains (with a domain-limited key Resend refuses the partner's emails; the test email shows it); no DNS ownership proof before the domain is created at Resend (claims end after 7 days instead); no "8 domains verified" alert yet (plan D6).
